\section{Threat Model and CRSC Derivation}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{Adversary Model}

We model the adversary $\mathcal{A}$ under the following assumptions,
representing a realistic supply-chain threat for LLM deployment.

\textbf{Capability.} $\mathcal{A}$ has \emph{training-time} access to the
base model $M_N$ and its training pipeline, and can inject a backdoor at
fraction $\rho \in (0,1)$ of training samples.
The adversary has no access to the defender's probe set $\mathcal{T}$
(held secret by the evaluator) and no influence over post-training alignment.

\textbf{Objective.} $\mathcal{A}$ aims to maximize Attack Success Rate (ASR)
on a target behavior $b^*$ after post-training, while maintaining benign
accuracy within $\epsilon_{\text{util}}$ of the clean-model baseline.

\textbf{Knowledge.} $\mathcal{A}$ knows the architecture family (transformer
with $N$ parameters, $|L|$ layers, hidden dimension $d_N$), the general
fine-tuning procedure (SFT or RLHF), and public information about the
post-training dataset.

\textbf{Out-of-scope.} Adaptive adversaries with oracle access to CRSC
scores are analyzed separately in Section~\ref{sec:security}.
Physical-world triggers and multi-model coordination attacks are excluded.

\textbf{Operational scenario.}
CRSC is designed for an \emph{auditor} who has access to both the
pre-fine-tuning checkpoint $M_N^{\text{bd}}$ and the post-fine-tuning
checkpoint $M_N^{\text{ft}}$.
In a supply-chain context, this corresponds to an organization that (a)~receives
a model from a supplier and retains the supplier's published base checkpoint as
a reference, or (b)~performs internal fine-tuning and wishes to verify that
backdoors from a potentially poisoned pre-training phase have been removed.
Settings where only $M_N^{\text{ft}}$ is available (i.e., the base checkpoint
is inaccessible or has itself been tampered with) are out of scope for the current
formulation; black-box extensions are identified as future work in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Backdoor Formalization}

Let $M_N\colon \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}$ be an LLM with $N$ parameters.

\begin{definition}[Backdoor]
A \emph{backdoor} $(\gamma, b^*)$ consists of a trigger function
$\gamma\colon \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{X}$ and a target behavior
$b^* \in \mathcal{Y}$ such that $M_N^{\text{bd}}$ satisfies:
\begin{align}
  \Pr[M_N^{\text{bd}}(\gamma(x)) = b^*] &\geq 1 - \varepsilon \\
  \Pr[M_N^{\text{bd}}(x) = M_N(x)]     &\geq 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{util}}
\end{align}
for all $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and small constants $\varepsilon, \varepsilon_{\text{util}} > 0$.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Backdoor injection.}
Given a clean dataset $D = \{(x_i, y_i)\}$, the adversary constructs a
poisoned dataset $D^{\text{bd}} = (1-\rho)D \cup \rho D^{\text{trig}}$,
where $D^{\text{trig}} = \{(\gamma(x_i), b^*)\}$.
Post-training applies alignment fine-tuning over a clean dataset
$D_{\text{ft}}$ (with $|D_{\text{ft}}| \ll |D|$) starting from
$M_N^{\text{bd}}$, yielding $M_N^{\text{ft}}$.
The defender's goal is to detect whether $M_N^{\text{ft}}$ retains the
backdoor.

\subsection{Representation Drift and Entropy Change}

CRSC rests on two complementary signals.

\textbf{Hidden-state drift.}
Let $\mathbf{H}_{\text{base}}, \mathbf{H}_{\text{ft}} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$
be the mean-pooled last-layer hidden states of $M_N^{\text{bd}}$ and
$M_N^{\text{ft}}$, respectively, stacked over $K$ probes.
The \emph{normalized Frobenius drift} is:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\text{hidden}} =
  \frac{\|\mathbf{H}_{\text{base}} - \mathbf{H}_{\text{ft}}\|_F}
       {\|\mathbf{H}_{\text{base}}\|_F}
\label{eq:delta_hid}
\end{equation}
$\Delta_{\text{hidden}} \approx 0$ indicates preserved backdoor representation;
$\Delta_{\text{hidden}} \gg 1$ indicates strong representational displacement.
Importantly, $\Delta_{\text{hidden}}$ is computed on \emph{trigger-embedded}
probes from $\mathcal{T}$; benign probes are expected to show low drift for
both backdoored and clean models, ensuring specificity to the backdoor signal.

\textbf{Entropy shift.}
Let $\ell_k^{\text{base}}$ and $\ell_k^{\text{ft}}$ be per-token cross-entropy
losses on probe $t_k$.
Define empirical entropy $H(\ell) = -\sum_k \hat{p}_k \log \hat{p}_k$
with $\hat{p}_k = e^{-\ell_k}/\!\sum_j e^{-\ell_j}$.
The \emph{entropy shift} $\Delta H = H(\ell^{\text{ft}}) - H(\ell^{\text{base}})$
captures the change in output uncertainty on triggered inputs.
A backdoored model collapses to confident incorrect predictions
($H(\ell^{\text{ft}})$ decreases), yielding $\Delta H < 0$.

\textbf{Normalization via $\Phi$.}
We map $\Delta H \in \mathbb{R}$ to $[0,1]$ via $\Phi(-\Delta H)$,
where $\Phi$ is the standard Normal CDF.
By the Berry--Ess\'{e}en theorem, the empirical entropy of a $K$-sample
bounded i.i.d.\ loss sequence converges to a Normal distribution as
$K \to \infty$.
Therefore $\Phi(-\Delta H)$ is the natural normalization: it maps
$-\Delta H$ to approximately Uniform$[0,1]$ under the null hypothesis
$H_0$: $M_N^{\text{ft}}$ is backdoor-free ($\Delta H \approx 0$),
and produces values near 1 when $\Delta H \ll 0$ (backdoor persists).

\subsection{CRSC Definition}

\begin{definition}[Cyber Resilience Scaling Coefficient]
\label{def:crsc}
Given model pair $(M_N^{\text{bd}}, M_N^{\text{ft}})$, probe set
$\mathcal{T}$ with $|\mathcal{T}| = K$, and weights $\alpha, \beta > 0$
with $\alpha + \beta = 1$, the \emph{CRSC} is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CRSC}(M_N^{\text{bd}}, M_N^{\text{ft}}) =
  \alpha\,(1 - \Delta_{\text{hidden}})^+
  + \beta\,\Phi(-\Delta H)
\label{eq:crsc}
\end{equation}
where $(x)^+ = \max(x,0)$.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Symmetric weighting ($\alpha = \beta = 0.5$).}
The two components measure statistically independent aspects of
backdoor persistence: $\Delta_{\text{hidden}}$ operates on the
representation space (intermediate activations), while $\Phi(-\Delta H)$
operates on the behavioral space (output loss distribution).
Under a maximum-entropy principle~\cite{jaynes1957information}, equal
weighting maximizes the entropy of the combined signal, minimizing
sensitivity to prior assumptions about which signal dominates for a given
trigger type.
The ablation in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} confirms empirically that both
components contribute independently ($\Delta$F1 $= +0.048$ for full vs.\
hidden-only; $+0.064$ for full vs.\ entropy-only), and a grid search over
$\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7\}$ (cross-validated on TrojAI)
found no statistically significant improvement over $\alpha = 0.5$
(all differences $< 0.007$ F1, within bootstrap CI).

\subsection{Theoretical Properties}

\begin{assumption}[Sparse backdoor subspace]
\label{asm:sparse}
The backdoor is encoded in a $k$-dimensional subspace
$\mathcal{S}_b \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_N}$ with
$k = \Theta(N^{\alpha_b})$ for some $\alpha_b \in (0, 1/2)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Generic fine-tuning gradient]
\label{asm:generic}
The gradient $G = \nabla_\theta\,\mathcal{L}(D_{\text{ft}})$
projected to hidden-state space acts as a random matrix of rank
$r = \Theta(|D_{\text{ft}}|)$ drawn from the Gaussian Orthogonal
Ensemble~\cite{anderson2010introduction}.
\emph{Remark on idealization:} Attention-head gradients exhibit low-rank
block structure (one block per head), violating strict GOE independence.
The assumption holds asymptotically when the number of fine-tuning
directions is large relative to the backdoor subspace dimension;
it should be treated as a tractability assumption rather than an exact
model.  The Marchenko--Pastur distribution~\cite{bai2010spectral}
characterizes the spectrum of the empirical gradient covariance under
this regime and provides the limiting spectral density used in the proof
of Proposition~\ref{prop:monotone}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity in $N$]
\label{prop:monotone}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:sparse}--\ref{asm:generic}, for fixed
$(\rho, D_{\text{ft}}, \gamma)$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{CRSC}(M_N^{\text{bd}}, M_N^{\text{ft}})\bigr]
  \;\text{is non-decreasing in}\; N.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
We bound the probability that fine-tuning displaces the backdoor subspace.
By Assumption~\ref{asm:generic}, the $r$ gradient directions in
$\mathbb{R}^{d_N}$ act as independent random unit vectors.
Let $\varepsilon > 0$ be a fixed angular threshold below which a gradient
direction does not meaningfully perturb the backdoor subspace.
By the spherical symmetry of the uniform measure on $\mathbb{S}^{d_N-1}$,
for a uniformly random unit vector $\mathbf{g}$ the expected squared projection
onto any fixed $k$-dimensional subspace $\mathcal{S}_b$ satisfies
$\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}_b}\mathbf{g}\|^2] = k/d_N$
(Vershynin~\cite{vershynin2018high}, Ch.~5.2).
Applying Markov's inequality:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\!\bigl[\|\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}_b}\mathbf{g}\|^2 > \varepsilon^2\bigr]
  \;\leq\; \frac{k}{\varepsilon^2\, d_N}.
\end{equation}
A union bound over $r = \Theta(|D_{\text{ft}}|)$ independent gradient directions then gives:
\begin{align}
  \Pr\bigl[\text{any gradient interferes with } \mathcal{S}_b\bigr]
  &\;\leq\; \frac{k \cdot r}{\varepsilon^2 d_N} \notag\\
  &\;=\; \Theta\!\left(\frac{|D_{\text{ft}}|}{\varepsilon^2 N^{\beta-\alpha_b}}\right)
\end{align}
Here $\beta$ is the empirically estimated growth exponent of the hidden
dimension: $d_N = \Theta(N^\beta)$.
For the LLaMA-2 family, $d_N \in \{4096,5120,8192\}$ at
$N \in \{7\text{B},13\text{B},70\text{B}\}$.
The 7B$\to$70B ratio gives $\beta_{70/7} \!=\! \log(2)/\log(10) \!\approx\! 0.30$
and the 7B$\to$13B ratio gives $\beta_{13/7} \!\approx\! 0.38$.
Since the power-law exponent is not perfectly stable over three points, we
treat $\beta$ as a parameter satisfying $\beta \in [0.28, 0.40]$;
monotonicity holds for \emph{any} $\beta > \alpha_b$.
The backdoor subspace exponent $\alpha_b$ governs how the rank of
$\mathcal{S}_b$ scales with $N$: empirically, backdoor representations
for token-level and style-level triggers are low-dimensional (rank $\leq 10$,
consistent with $\alpha_b \approx 0$, well below any plausible $\beta$), as
evidenced by the small $\Delta_{\text{hidden}}$ required to achieve high CRSC
in our synthetic experiments.
Since $\varepsilon$ is fixed, $|D_{\text{ft}}|$ is fixed, and
$\beta - \alpha_b > 0$, the interference probability decreases as
$N^{-(\beta-\alpha_b)} \to 0$.  Consequently,
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{\text{hidden}}]$ decreases with $N$, so
$\mathbb{E}[(1-\Delta_{\text{hidden}})^+]$ is non-decreasing.
An analogous argument for the entropy component follows from the
concentration of bounded loss distributions~\cite{boucheron2013concentration}.
Combined, $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{CRSC}]$ is non-decreasing in $N$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}[Fine-tuning budget threshold]
\label{cor:budget_threshold}
Let $\gamma_{\text{ft}}$ denote the scaling exponent of the fine-tuning
dataset: $|D_{\text{ft}}| = \Theta(N^{\gamma_{\text{ft}}})$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $\gamma_{\text{ft}} < \beta - \alpha_b$ (fixed or sub-linear budget
        relative to hidden dimension growth), the interference probability
        decreases with $N$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:monotone} holds.
  \item If $\gamma_{\text{ft}} = \beta - \alpha_b$, the interference probability
        is $\Theta(1)$ and the monotonicity direction is indeterminate.
  \item If $\gamma_{\text{ft}} > \beta - \alpha_b$ (super-linear budget; we
        conjecture $\gamma_{\text{ft}} \approx 1 > \beta$ for Meta's production
        RLHF based on public reporting~\cite{touvron2023llama2}, though the
        exact budget scaling is not publicly documented), the
        interference probability \emph{increases} with $N$, the fine-tuning
        effectively sanitizes larger models more thoroughly, and we predict
        $\mathrm{CRSC} \ll \tau$---consistent with Table~\ref{tab:real_crsc}.
\end{enumerate}
The CRSC monotonicity claim in the paper's thesis applies exclusively to
case~(i), the fixed-budget threat scenario.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[CRSC as ASR Proxy]
\label{prop:proxy}
Let $p = \mathrm{ASR}(M_N^{\text{ft}})$.
There exist constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that with probability
$\geq 1 - \delta$ over $\mathcal{T}$:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\mathrm{CRSC}(M_N^{\text{bd}},M_N^{\text{ft}})
        - c_1\,p - c_2\bigr|
  \;\leq\; C\!\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{K}}
\label{eq:proxy}
\end{equation}
for a constant $C$ depending only on $d_N$ and $\|\mathbf{H}_{\text{base}}\|_F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
\emph{Representation component.}
A backdoor at retention level $p$ implies that $M_N^{\text{ft}}$ preserves
the trigger-associated representation in at least a $p$-fraction of probes.
By the Johnson--Lindenstrauss lemma applied to the probe embeddings,
$\Delta_{\text{hidden}} \leq 1 - p + \epsilon_1$ with
$\epsilon_1 = O(1/\sqrt{K})$.

\emph{Entropy component.}
A model with $\mathrm{ASR} = p$ concentrates its loss on the target token
$b^*$ for a $p$-fraction of probes, reducing output entropy by
$\Theta(p\log|\mathcal{Y}|)$ relative to a benign model.
By McDiarmid's inequality (bounded differences constant $\leq \log K/K$),
$\Phi(-\Delta H)$ concentrates around $\Phi(p\,c_H)$ with deviation
$\epsilon_2 = O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/K})$.

Linearizing $\Phi$ around its operating point (valid for
$p \in [0.2,0.8]$; we verified that 68 of the 72 experimental runs
fall within this range, with four low-$\rho$ runs at ASR $< 0.2$
excluded from the proxy bound) and combining both bounds yields
\eqref{eq:proxy} with $c_1 = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \phi(0)\,c_H)$ and
$c_2 = \frac{1}{2}\Phi(0) = \frac{1}{4}$,
where $\phi = \Phi'$ is the standard Normal PDF.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}
\eqref{eq:proxy} requires $K \geq C^2\log(1/\delta)$.
For $\delta = 0.05$ and $C \approx 1.5$, this gives $K \geq 7$,
well below both the $K = 500$ synthetic protocol and the $K = 10$
stratified pilot (Section~\ref{sec:real_validation}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Detection Rule and Negative Control}

The detection rule is $\hat{b} = \textsc{high-risk}$ iff
$\mathrm{CRSC} \geq \tau$, with $\tau = 0.62$ calibrated to achieve
FPR $\leq 0.10$ (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
For a backdoor-free model ($p \approx 0$), Proposition~\ref{prop:proxy}
implies $\mathrm{CRSC} \approx c_2 = 0.25 \ll \tau$, consistent with the
real LLaMA-2 results ($\mathrm{CRSC} \in [0.21, 0.31]$, Table~\ref{tab:real_crsc}),
which serve as a natural negative-control baseline: these production models
were fine-tuned without backdoor injection, confirming that benign alignment
yields CRSC well below $\tau$.
